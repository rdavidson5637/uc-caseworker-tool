UNIVERSAL CREDIT - WHAT YOU'LL GET (PAYMENTS)
Source: gov.uk/universal-credit/what-youll-get (retrieved 7 August 2026)

Universal Credit is paid monthly. How much you get depends on:
- your standard allowance
- any extra amounts that apply to you
- any money taken off your payment
- if you are working, how much you earn

STANDARD ALLOWANCE

You get one standard allowance for your household.
- Single and under 25: £338.58 per month
- Single and 25 or over: £424.90 per month
- Live with partner, both under 25: £528.34 per month (for both)
- Live with partner, either of you 25 or over: £666.97 per month (for both)

EXTRA AMOUNTS

You may get more money on top of your standard allowance if you are eligible. You need to report changes when you become eligible for an extra amount. They are not added automatically. For example, if you become a carer you need to tell DWP. The carer's element is not automatically applied if you start getting Carer's Allowance.

IF YOU HAVE CHILDREN

You get £303.94 a month for each child that lives with you. If your first child was born before 6 April 2017, you get an extra £47.94 a month for that child.

You get money for each child until the 31 August after their:
- 16th birthday
- 19th birthday, if they are in eligible education or training (for example GCSEs, A levels, BTECs, Scottish Highers, SVQs or NVQs up to level 3)

IF YOUR CHILD HAS A DISABILITY

You might get an extra monthly amount if any of your children are disabled. You get either:
- £164.79 - the lower amount
- £514.71 - the higher amount

The higher amount is for a child who gets the highest rate of the care component of DLA for children, the higher rate of the daily living part of PIP, or is blind or severely sight impaired. The lower amount is for a child who gets any other rate of either DLA for children or PIP.

CHILDCARE COSTS

You can claim back up to 85% of your childcare costs if you are working. If you live with your partner both of you need to be working, unless one of you is unable to work due to a disability or health condition.

The childcare needs to be from a registered provider. The most you can get each month is:
- £1,071.09 for one child
- £1,836.16 for 2 or more children

You need to pay your childcare costs up front and claim the money back as part of your payment. Support is available to help pay childcare costs up front - claimants should talk to their work coach.

IF YOU HAVE A DISABILITY OR HEALTH CONDITION

You might get an extra monthly amount if you cannot work because of a health condition, disability or long-term illness:
- Severe health condition or disability unlikely to change, or nearing end of life: £429.80
- Condition assessed as less severe or may improve over time: £217.26

Different rules apply if the health condition was reported before 6 April 2026.

If you live with your partner and you both have limited capability for work and work-related activity, you only get one extra monthly amount (the higher one if one of you is eligible for it).

CARER'S ELEMENT

You could get an extra amount if you care for someone who gets a qualifying disability benefit (including Attendance Allowance, DLA middle or highest care rate, PIP daily living part, Adult Disability Payment, Child Disability Payment middle or highest care award, Armed Forces Independence Payment, or Constant Attendance Allowance at qualifying rates).

You need to provide care for at least 35 hours a week. The extra monthly amount is £209.34. If you live with your partner and you both care for the same person, you only get one extra amount.

If you are getting an extra amount for limited capability for work and work-related activity (LCWRA), you cannot also get the carer's element.

HOUSING COSTS

You could get money to help pay your housing costs, covering rent and some service charges. Homeowners might be able to get a loan to help with mortgage interest payments.

MONEY TAKEN OFF YOUR PAYMENT

Your payments might be reduced if any of the following apply:
- you are paying back an advance on a Universal Credit payment
- you would get above the amount limited by the benefit cap
- you have been overpaid benefits in the past
- you owe money for Council Tax, court fines, electricity, gas, water or Child Maintenance
- you pay your gas or electricity bill directly from your Universal Credit payment
- you have a paid job
- you have other income, for example money from pensions or certain other benefits
- you have more than £6,000 in money, savings and investments

If you have over £6,000 in money, savings and investments, your payment is reduced by £4.35 for every £250 you have between £6,000 and £16,000, plus another £4.35 for any remaining amount that is not a complete £250.

BENEFITS THAT REDUCE UNIVERSAL CREDIT POUND FOR POUND

Your Universal Credit payment is reduced by an amount equal to the payment of: Armed Forces Pensions, Carer's Allowance, Carer Support Payment (Scotland), Incapacity Benefit, Industrial Injuries Disablement Benefit (with some exclusions), Maternity Allowance, New Style Employment and Support Allowance, New Style Jobseeker's Allowance, Severe Disablement Allowance, State Pension, Widowed Mother's Allowance, or Widowed Parent's Allowance.

MOVING TO UNIVERSAL CREDIT FROM OTHER BENEFITS

If you have applied for Universal Credit, you keep getting your current benefit paid for 2 more weeks (income-related ESA or Housing Benefit only), provided you are still eligible. You do not need to pay back these extra payments and they do not affect your Universal Credit.

If you received a Migration Notice letter, you might also get a 'transitional protection' payment.

CLAIM REVIEWS

Your claim might be reviewed to make sure you are getting the right payment and support.
