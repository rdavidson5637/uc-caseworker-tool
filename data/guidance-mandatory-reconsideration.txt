CHALLENGE A BENEFIT DECISION (MANDATORY RECONSIDERATION)
Source: gov.uk/mandatory-reconsideration (retrieved 7 August 2026)

ELIGIBILITY

If you disagree with a decision about benefits or child maintenance you can ask for the decision to be looked at again - this is called 'mandatory reconsideration'.

It is free to ask for mandatory reconsideration.

You can ask for mandatory reconsideration if any of the following apply:
- you think the office dealing with your claim has made an error or missed important evidence
- you disagree with the reasons for the decision
- you want to have the decision looked at again

Some decisions cannot be reconsidered. Others can go straight to an appeal. The original decision letter will say if this applies.

TIME LIMIT

You usually need to ask for mandatory reconsideration within one month of the date of the decision. You can ask for it after one month if you have a good reason, for example if you have been in hospital or had a bereavement.

BENEFITS THIS APPLIES TO

Mandatory reconsideration applies to benefits including: Attendance Allowance, Bereavement Allowance, Carer's Allowance, Carer's Credit, child maintenance, Compensation Recovery Scheme (including NHS recovery claims), Diffuse Mesothelioma Payment Scheme, Disability Living Allowance (DLA), Employment and Support Allowance (ESA), Funeral Expenses Payment, Industrial Injuries Disablement Benefit, Jobseeker's Allowance (JSA), Maternity Allowance, National Insurance credits, Pension Credit, Personal Independence Payment (PIP), Sure Start Maternity Grant, Universal Credit (including advance payments), and Winter Fuel Payment.

There are different processes to challenge decisions about Budgeting Loans, Child Benefit, Guardian's Allowance, Housing Benefit, Social Security Scotland benefits, Tax-Free Childcare and 30 hours free childcare, and Vaccine Damage Payment.

THE PROCESS - STEP BY STEP

Step 1: Find support and advice. Free support and advice is available from Citizens Advice, Advicenow, and advisers listed on Advicelocal. You can also seek advice from a legal adviser or solicitor.

Step 2: Ask for mandatory reconsideration. You normally need to do this within one month of the date on your decision letter. For Universal Credit, you can do this by putting a note in your journal, over the phone, face to face or in writing.

Step 3: Appeal the decision to a tribunal. If you are unhappy with the decision after mandatory reconsideration, you can appeal it to the Social Security and Child Support Tribunal.

Step 4: Ask for the tribunal's decision to be 'set aside' (cancelled) if you disagree with it. The decision letter from the tribunal will explain how. Alternatively, if you think the decision was wrong for a legal reason, you can appeal to the Upper Tribunal (Administrative Appeals Chamber). You may be eligible for legal aid to help you appeal to the Upper Tribunal.
