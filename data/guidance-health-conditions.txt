HEALTH CONDITIONS, DISABILITY AND UNIVERSAL CREDIT
Source: gov.uk/health-conditions-disability-universal-credit (retrieved 7 August 2026)
Note: the brief's original URL gov.uk/universal-credit/if-you-have-a-disability-or-health-condition now redirects; this is the current GOV.UK guide.

OVERVIEW

Universal Credit is a monthly payment to help with your living costs. You might get an extra amount of Universal Credit if you have a health condition or disability that limits how much work you can do. DWP refers to this as your 'capability to work'.

Your monthly payment is based on your circumstances, for example your health condition or disability, income and housing costs.

If you have a health condition or disability, you will have an assessment to work out how much Universal Credit you can get and what you need to do in return. This may be a Work Capability Assessment - claimants are told if they need one after they claim.

The guide covers:
- reporting your health condition or disability
- what happens if you have a Work Capability Assessment
- working and claiming Universal Credit
- claiming Universal Credit if you are nearing the end of life
- what happens if you get the severe disability premium

OTHER SUPPORT

You may also be eligible for New Style Employment and Support Allowance. Other financial support may be available depending on circumstances.

IF YOU ARE NEARING THE END OF LIFE

If you are nearing the end of life (for example, due to a life-limiting illness) you might be able to get extra money for Universal Credit.

RELATED EXTRA AMOUNTS (see payments guidance for current rates)

- Severe health condition or disability unlikely to change, or nearing end of life: higher extra monthly amount
- Condition assessed as less severe or may improve over time: lower extra monthly amount

Different rules apply if the health condition was reported before 6 April 2026. If both partners in a couple have limited capability for work and work-related activity, only one extra monthly amount is paid.
